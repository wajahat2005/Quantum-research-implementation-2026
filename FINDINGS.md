## 1. Scope & Research Objective

This research investigation conducts an independent, statistically rigorous, and mathematically grounded audit and cross-dataset benchmark of eight landmark publications in Quantum Machine Learning (QML) and Healthcare AI (2025–2026). All evaluations are conducted strictly within the clinical domain of **diabetes diagnosis, risk screening, and 30-day inpatient readmission**:

1. **D1: Pima Indians Diabetes Database** ($N=768, d=8$) — Type 2 diabetes screening.
2. **D2: Early Stage Diabetes Risk Prediction, Sylhet (UCI 529)** ($N=520, d=16$) — Symptom-based risk questionnaire.
3. **D3: CDC Diabetes Health Indicators (BRFSS 2015, UCI 891)** ($N=253,680, d=21$) — Large-scale epidemiological survey.
4. **D4: UCI Diabetes 130-US Hospitals 1999–2008 (UCI 296)** ($N=101,766, d=50$) — 30-day clinical readmission.

### Explicit Scope Exclusions
- **Non-Diabetes Datasets Excluded**: Breast Cancer Wisconsin Diagnostic (WDBC), METABRIC, and Parkinson's Disease (UCI 174) are strictly **out of scope** and were neither loaded nor evaluated.
- **Domain Adaptations**: Non-diabetes source datasets in surveyed papers (e.g., ISCX-URL2016 network traffic in Sodar et al., private Huawei acoustic speech data in Zhang et al.) were adapted to clinical diabetes data by applying their exact mathematical formulation (e.g., graph intuitionistic fuzzy weighting, amplitude vs. angle quantum kernel encodings) directly to diabetes benchmarks.

## 6. Negative Results & Refutations

1. **Absence of Quantum Advantage on Clinical Tabular Data**: Across all 4 clinical diabetes datasets, state-of-the-art classical models (LightGBM, ExtraTrees, Classical Stacking) consistently outperform or match all quantum kernels (Angle, ZZ, Z, Pauli) and VQCs. Classical models train $100\times$ to $10,000\times$ faster.
2. **Failure of Amplitude Encoding in Medicine**: Amplitude encoding's mandatory $L_2$ unit normalization erases absolute physiological scales (e.g., blood glucose 180 vs 90 mg/dL). This causes complete sensitivity collapse (0.00% recall), making amplitude encoding unacceptable for medical diagnostics.
3. **Harm of Parameterized Quantum Entanglement**: Entangling ZZ-feature maps and Pauli maps underperform unentangled Z-maps and Angle maps on clinical tabular data. High-frequency phase over-rotation fragments decision boundaries in low-dimensional spaces.
4. **"Quantum Random Forest" is a Misnomer**: The "QRF" in Upama et al. contains no quantum hardware, quantum gates, or quantum algorithms; it is simply a depth-capped classical random forest.
5. **DiaBeats AUC 1.00 is a Mathematical Impossibility**: Mann-Whitney U theorem proves that the published 25 test errors bound $\text{AUC}_{\max} \le 0.9700$.

## 7. Limitations & Reproducibility Guide

### Limitations
- Quantum simulations were conducted via exact statevector simulators on CPU. While noise-free statevectors provide an upper bound on quantum performance, physical NISQ hardware (with gate errors, decoherence, and measurement shot noise) would perform worse.
- Gram matrix computation scales as $O(N^2)$, requiring stratified subsampling for very large cohorts like CDC BRFSS ($N=253,680$).
